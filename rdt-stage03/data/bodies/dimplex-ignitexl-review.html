The Dimplex IgniteXL is a strong fit for buyers who want a wide, contemporary fireplace without the depth, venting, fuel line, or routine cleanup of a gas or wood unit. Its defining advantage is architectural efficiency: the firebox is only 5 3/4 inches deep, yet it creates a long horizontal focal point in four widths. The lineup includes the XLF50, XLF60, XLF74, and XLF100.

Quick verdict: who should buy the Dimplex IgniteXL?

The IgniteXL is best for a design-led room where a slim, single-sided linear fireplace is the goal. It works especially well in media walls, bedrooms, living rooms, finished basements, condos, and commercial reception or hospitality spaces where a clean built-in appearance matters more than a deep traditional firebox. Because the flame effect can operate without heat, it can remain a visual feature throughout the year.

It is less suitable when the project requires a corner or three-sided viewing configuration, a taller flame presentation, or primary space heating. Those requirements point toward a different fireplace family. Buyers should also plan the electrical connection and finished wall assembly before construction begins; although the firebox is shallow, a polished installation still depends on accurate framing and finish coordination.

Flame presentation and visual controls

The IgniteXL uses Dimplex’s Multi-Fire flame effect with an acrylic crystal media bed. The long, low opening creates a distinctly modern appearance rather than the taller proportions of a conventional firebox. Flame and media lighting can be adjusted, and the manufacturer describes multiple color themes, brightness settings, and prism-style color cycling. The result can shift from a warmer, restrained presentation to a more colorful contemporary look.

That flexibility is useful in rooms that serve more than one purpose. A subdued flame setting can support everyday television viewing, while brighter or more colorful settings create a stronger evening focal point. The included remote and hidden touch controls operate the unit. Unlike newer app-connected Dimplex families, the standard IgniteXL’s current product literature emphasizes the remote and touch controls rather than Flame Connect app control.

The flame effect operates independently of the heater. This is important for warm climates and shoulder seasons: buyers can use the visual effect without adding heat to the room.

Heating performance: 120V versus 240V

Dimplex lists the IgniteXL for 120V or 240V operation. At 120V, the published rating is 5,118 BTU; at 240V, it is 8,530 BTU. The higher-voltage connection therefore matters when supplemental heat is an important part of the project. Dimplex markets its Comfort$aver system as on-demand supplemental heating for spaces up to 1,000 square feet, but real-world comfort depends on ceiling height, insulation, outdoor temperature, room openness, and other heat loss.

Treat the IgniteXL as zone or supplemental heat, not as a universal replacement for a properly sized primary HVAC system. A 240V connection increases available output, but it does not change the fireplace’s visual dimensions or make a poorly insulated room heat like a closed, well-insulated one.

The owner’s manual recommends a dedicated, properly fused 15-amp circuit rated for the selected voltage. Electrical work, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, disconnects, and any ground-fault requirements must comply with the current manual and local electrical code. Have a qualified electrician review the plan before the wall is closed.

Installation planning before framing

The 5 3/4-inch firebox depth is one of the IgniteXL’s most useful characteristics. Dimplex’s current brochure provides framing references for both 2×4 and 2×6 construction, which makes the series practical for many shallow feature-wall assemblies. Even so, the fireplace should not be treated as a finish-only decision.

Coordinate these items before framing:

  • The exact model and its current installation manual.
  • The finished wall width, height, and cladding thickness.
  • The electrical voltage, circuit, wire route, and connection location.
  • The manufacturer’s clearance and airflow requirements.
  • The weight and support requirements of the television, mantel, stone, tile, or millwork above it.
  • Service access for the fireplace and electrical components.
  • The final relationship among the viewing opening, television, seating, and sight lines.

The fireplace requires no chimney, flue, or gas line, but “no venting” does not mean “no installation planning.” The heater’s air path cannot be obstructed, the electrical connection must remain code-compliant, and finish materials should be detailed so the unit can be installed and serviced as the manufacturer directs.

Can an IgniteXL go below a television?

An electric fireplace and television can share a feature wall, but the layout must be designed from the manual outward. Confirm the fireplace’s heat outlet, the television manufacturer’s temperature and clearance requirements, the mounting hardware, and the wall’s material stack-up. A mantel or heat-management detail should never be improvised as a substitute for the required clearances.

Comfort also matters. Mounting the television too high is a common consequence of stacking a fireplace, mantel, soundbar, and screen without drawing the full elevation first. Use the exact fireplace height, the desired bottom-of-screen height, and the viewing distance to test the composition. If the stack becomes too tall, a lower-profile finish detail, a different screen location, or a more compact fireplace size may produce a better room.

IgniteXL strengths and tradeoffs

Strengths: The series offers four useful widths, a shallow body, year-round flame operation, a modern acrylic crystal media bed, adjustable visual settings, remote and touch controls, and higher supplemental heat output when connected at 240V. The consistent 16 1/2-inch product height across the lineup also makes it easier to scale a design up or down without changing its overall visual language.

Tradeoffs: The single-sided, low-profile opening will not suit every aesthetic. Buyers seeking a taller, deeper, or multi-sided presentation should compare other Dimplex families. The heater is supplemental, and full performance depends on the electrical plan. The model also lacks the app-centered control experience highlighted on newer Ignite families. Finally, stone, tile, millwork, electrical labor, framing, television mounting, and service-access details can represent a meaningful part of total project cost beyond the firebox itself.

IgniteXL alternatives worth comparing

The IgniteXL remains compelling when shallow depth, a clean single-sided view, and four broad size options lead the brief. Dimplex IgniteXL FAQs

Which Dimplex IgniteXL size should I choose?

Choose from the finished wall composition, not the room’s square footage alone. The XLF50 works well on narrower walls and in bedrooms or condos; the XLF60 fits many standard living-room elevations; the XLF74 provides a stronger presence on wide media walls; and the XLF100 is intended for very broad statement walls. Tape the exact outside dimensions on the wall and verify the framing opening before ordering.

Does the IgniteXL require 240V power?

The current product specifications list both 120V and 240V operation. The published heat output is 5,118 BTU at 120V and 8,530 BTU at 240V. If maximum supplemental heat matters, plan for the 240V connection. A qualified electrician should confirm the circuit and installation against the current manual and local code.

Can the Dimplex IgniteXL operate without heat?

Yes. The flame effect can operate independently of the heater, so the fireplace can provide visual ambiance in warm weather or whenever additional heat is not wanted.

Does the IgniteXL need a chimney or vent?

No chimney, flue, or gas vent is required. The unit is electric. Its heater airflow and installation clearances still must remain unobstructed, and the electrical connection must follow the manual and local code.

Can I install an IgniteXL under a TV?

It can be incorporated into many media-wall designs, but the answer depends on the exact fireplace manual, television requirements, wall assembly, heat outlet, clearances, and mounting plan. Draw the complete elevation before framing and avoid mounting the screen higher than is comfortable for the seating position.

Who should install the fireplace?

The framing and finish work should be completed by people familiar with the selected wall system, and the electrical connection should be reviewed or performed by a qualified electrician. Follow the manual supplied with the exact model. If the fireplace, television, or finish assembly is complex, coordinate the trades before the wall is closed.

Is the IgniteXL a primary room heater?

It is best treated as supplemental or zone heat. Dimplex markets the Comfort$aver system for supplemental heating up to 1,000 square feet, but actual results vary with the building and climate. Primary heating decisions should be based on a proper load calculation, not the fireplace’s marketing area alone.
