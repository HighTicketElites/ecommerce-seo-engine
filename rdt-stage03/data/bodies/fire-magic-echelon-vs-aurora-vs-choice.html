Fire Magic Echelon, Aurora, and Choice grills share the same basic purpose: giving an outdoor kitchen a durable, built-in gas-grilling platform. They do not serve the same buyer. Echelon is the flagship family, Aurora preserves many premium conveniences with a simpler control package, and Choice concentrates spending on the core cooking system. The right series depends less on which name sits highest in the lineup and more on which features you will actually use.

Fire Magic Echelon vs Aurora vs Choice: the short answer

Choose Echelon when you want Fire Magic's most feature-rich platform, including digital temperature information, premium lighting and ignition, and a configuration that can bundle infrared searing and rotisserie cooking. It is the strongest fit for a high-use luxury kitchen where the grill is expected to be the main cooking appliance.

Choose Aurora when you want a premium Fire Magic build and a large cooking surface but prefer traditional analog temperature monitoring. The selected A790i keeps the same 36-by-22-inch main cooking area as the E790i used here while removing some higher-cost electronics and included specialty-cooking equipment.

Choose Choice when the goal is a dependable built-in Fire Magic grill at the most approachable position in the brand's lineup. The C650i is still a substantial 36-inch, three-burner grill, but it uses a smaller cooking area, analog monitoring, continuous-spark ignition, and a simpler convenience package.

Series hierarchy and ideal buyer

Fire Magic presents Echelon as its flagship line, Aurora as a premium family carrying many of the brand's desirable features, and Choice as its most affordable series. That hierarchy is helpful, but it should not become a reflexive buying decision. A buyer who cooks steaks weekly may value an included infrared burner more than a buyer who mainly grills vegetables and fish. Someone who entertains after dark may care more about lighting than a homeowner whose cooking happens in daylight.

Start by identifying the non-negotiable cooking functions, then choose the least expensive series and configuration that supplies them. That approach prevents paying for a digital display, rotisserie system, or lighting package that will rarely be used. It also prevents underbuying a simpler grill and later discovering that a desired feature cannot be added cleanly.

Echelon Diamond: the flagship experience

The {{title:fire-magic-echelon-diamond-e790i-36-inch-built-in-natural-gas-grill-with-one-infrared-burner-rotisserie-digital-thermometer-e790i-8l1n}} is built for buyers who want the broadest cooking toolkit in one grill. Its 36-by-22-inch main surface provides 792 square inches of primary cooking area, while the warming rack adds 288 square inches. The current configuration uses three main burner positions, with one supplied as an infrared burner for high-temperature searing.

The digital display is an important Echelon differentiator. It gives the cook more information than a lid-mounted analog dial and supports a more measurement-driven approach to repeatable results. The selected unit also includes a rotisserie system and a 13,000-BTU backburner, allowing slow rotation and rear-fired roasting without relying only on heat from below.

Echelon makes the most sense when those capabilities are part of the actual cooking routine. It is compelling for a homeowner who regularly prepares steaks, roasts, whole poultry, mixed menus, or large gatherings and wants multiple cooking modes available without swapping appliances. It is less compelling if the grill will be used mainly for occasional burgers and vegetables, where Aurora or Choice may preserve the experience that matters at a lower investment.

Aurora: premium fundamentals with simpler controls

Its main cooking surface is also 36 by 22 inches, giving it the same 792-square-inch primary area and 288-square-inch warming area as the matched Echelon. That makes Aurora especially attractive to buyers who need E790-scale capacity but do not need Echelon's digital control package.

The selected A790i uses three conventional burners rated at a combined 90,000 BTU. Aurora is often the value sweet spot for a substantial luxury outdoor kitchen. It retains a large cooking area and nighttime convenience while keeping operation familiar through an analog thermometer. Choose it when capacity and durable daily grilling matter more than digital monitoring or an included infrared-and-rotisserie package.

Choice: the essential Fire Magic platform

The {{title:fire-magic-choice-c650i-36-inch-built-in-natural-gas-grill-with-analog-thermometer-c650i-rt1n}} is not simply a smaller-feature Echelon. It is a deliberately simpler platform for buyers who want Fire Magic construction and core gas-grilling performance without paying for every premium convenience.

The C650i uses three tubular stainless-steel burners with a combined rating of 69,000 BTU and provides 648 square inches of primary cooking area. An analog thermometer and continuous-spark ignition keep the control system straightforward. Compared with the two A790/E790 models, this selected Choice unit omits conveniences such as internal and external lighting and heat separators.

Choice fits a project where the grill budget must coexist with cabinetry, refrigeration, ventilation, counters, plumbing, and other outdoor-kitchen expenses. It can also suit an experienced cook who prefers simple controls and does not view electronics or specialty burners as essential. The tradeoff is not only fewer features; the matched model also gives up some cooking capacity, so confirm that 648 square inches is comfortable for the household's typical guest count.

Cooking capacity and entertaining

Echelon E790i and Aurora A790i are closely matched on physical cooking capacity. Both provide a 792-square-inch main surface plus a 288-square-inch warming rack in the configurations evaluated here. That shared size makes the decision primarily about cooking systems and controls rather than about how much food fits at once.

Choice C650i's 648-square-inch main surface remains generous for many households, but the difference becomes more noticeable when managing several heat zones, cooking for a crowd, or preparing proteins and sides simultaneously. More area allows greater separation between direct and indirect zones. It can also provide room to move food away from flare-ups without taking it off the grill.

Do not choose by a theoretical maximum burger count alone. Think about the largest realistic menu: steaks plus vegetables, a roast with an indirect zone, or several foods finishing at different times. Then account for the warming rack and any griddle, smoker, pizza, or rotisserie accessories expected to share the outdoor kitchen.

Searing, rotisserie, and specialty cooking

The exact Echelon configuration has the clearest advantage for specialty techniques. One infrared main burner supports intense direct searing, while the rotisserie and rear burner create a dedicated roasting system. That combination can reduce the number of separate accessories needed to handle steaks, poultry, and larger cuts.

The selected Aurora product is intentionally more conventional: three standard main burners, without an included rear burner or rotisserie package. That does not make it incapable of varied cooking. Multi-zone gas grilling, indirect heat, roasting under the hood, and accessory-based techniques remain possible. It does mean a buyer expecting rotisserie equipment should either choose a differently equipped Aurora model or include the correct compatible components in the project plan.

Choice concentrates on traditional grilling. It is appropriate when most meals rely on direct and indirect heat across conventional burners. Before assuming a future upgrade path, verify accessory compatibility for the exact C650i model. Grill families often share visual design while using different openings, brackets, electrical requirements, and model-specific parts.

Controls, ignition, and nighttime use

Echelon's digital system is for cooks who value at-a-glance information and a more technology-forward interface. Aurora and Choice use analog temperature monitoring, which is familiar, simple, and sufficient for many cooking styles. A separate probe thermometer can provide food-level readings regardless of the grill's built-in display.

The selected Echelon and Aurora use hot-surface ignition and require planned electrical service. Choice uses continuous-spark ignition and a simpler feature set. Ignition type affects more than the first startup: it influences electrical coordination, service access, and how the appliance integrates into the island. Confirm the exact voltage, circuit, grounding, and shutoff requirements in the manual before construction.

Lighting also matters in a real outdoor kitchen. Aurora retains internal and external lights, while the matched Choice configuration does not. If the kitchen will be used after sunset, evaluate both the grill's lights and the site's task lighting. General patio lighting behind the cook can cast shadows over the grates, so the lighting plan should be tested from the cooking position.

Installation, fuel, and cutout planning

All three products in this comparison are built-in natural-gas models, but their openings are not interchangeable. The current Fire Magic documentation for the Echelon E790i identifies a cutout of approximately 37 inches wide, 23.5 inches deep, and 11.5 inches high. The matched Choice C650i listing identifies a different opening of approximately 37.75 inches wide, 19.5 inches deep, and 11.5 inches high. That depth difference alone is enough to disrupt a prefabricated island or finished stone opening.

Before fabrication, lock the exact model number, fuel, gas supply, regulator arrangement, ventilation, combustible clearances, access panels, electrical supply, and countertop overhang. Use the installation document shipped for that exact unit and have the appliance physically available for final verification whenever possible. Product-family names and nominal widths are not construction drawings.

Natural gas is convenient for a fixed kitchen supplied by the property, while propane can work where a permanent gas line is impractical. Fuel conversion should never be assumed. Order the correct factory fuel configuration or use only a manufacturer-authorized conversion process when one exists. A qualified gas professional should size and test the line based on the total connected load and local code.

Frequently asked questions

Is Echelon always better than Aurora?

No. Echelon offers a more advanced feature package, but Aurora can be the better purchase when the buyer does not need digital controls, infrared searing, or an included rotisserie system. In the matched products, Aurora provides the same main cooking-area dimensions as Echelon.

What is the main difference between Aurora and Choice?

The matched Aurora is larger in cooking area and adds hot-surface ignition, internal and external lighting, and heat separators. The Choice C650i uses a smaller 648-square-inch main surface, analog monitoring, continuous-spark ignition, and a simpler convenience package.

Do all Echelon grills include an infrared burner?

Do not assume they do. The E790i linked here is specifically configured with one infrared main burner. Fire Magic products can be offered with different burner and accessory packages, so verify the complete model code and listed equipment.

Can I add a rotisserie to Aurora later?

Some models support compatible rotisserie components, but fit and included parts vary. Confirm the exact grill model and manufacturer-approved kit before purchase rather than assuming a universal accessory will work.

Are the three grills the same size because they are called 36-inch models?

No. Nominal width does not guarantee identical appliance or cutout dimensions. The current E790i and C650i documentation already shows different required depths. Use the exact installation drawing for the selected model before building the island.

Which series is best for a small household?

Household size is only one factor. Choice can provide ample capacity for many smaller households, while a serious cook may still prefer Echelon's specialized tools. Base the decision on typical menus, entertaining frequency, and preferred techniques.

Do I need electricity for a built-in Fire Magic grill?

The requirement depends on the exact model and its ignition, lighting, and control systems. The matched Echelon and Aurora configurations require electrical coordination. Follow the current manual and have a qualified professional plan the circuit and grounding.

Should I choose natural gas or propane?

Natural gas is convenient for a fixed kitchen with a properly sized supply line. Propane can suit sites without that utility connection. Choose the correct factory fuel configuration, verify total BTU demand, and have the installation leak-tested under applicable code.

Where should I start if I am not sure?

List the largest meal you realistically cook, the techniques you use, and the features you would miss. Then compare the exact models, not only the series names. Resideterra can help verify fuel, configuration, accessories, opening dimensions, and current availability before fabrication.
